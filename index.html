Vegetation Dynamics under Mining and Monsoon Variability: Temporal and Spatial Study of the Singareni Coalfields, Telangana

Twenty-two years of dry-season Landsat imagery around nine Singareni Collieries leases show the land getting greener. This portal documents how the record was built, what it shows, and why the strong link with coal production turned out to be a shared time trend rather than a cause.

What the study found

  • The land greened

    Mean NDVI rose in all three distance zones from 2005 to 2026. Mann–Kendall gave Kendall's τ of 0.57 to 0.65 (p ≤ 0.0003), and Sen's slope was 0.0024 to 0.0026 NDVI per year. The 2022–2026 average was 27 to 32% above the 2005–2009 average.

  • The mines stayed browner

    The 0–1 km zone, which contains the leases, had the lowest NDVI in 19 of 22 years and the least consistent trend.

  • Climate did not trend

    Monsoon rainfall and temperature had no significant trend, so they cannot explain a 20-year rise. Previous-monsoon rainfall was only weakly related to NDVI (r = 0.33 to 0.37, not significant).

  • Production was a false lead

    Raw NDVI anomalies correlated with opencast production at r = 0.70 to 0.76. Both series rise over time. After removing the linear trend from each, the correlation fell to between −0.22 and −0.28 and was no longer significant.

Abstract

Vegetation around an opencast coal mine responds both to mining and to the south-west monsoon. This work builds a 22-year record for nine SCCL leases between Ramagundam and Mandamarri in Telangana. One cloud-free January or February Landsat 5, 7, 8 or 9 surface reflectance scene was used for each year from 2005 to 2026, and NDVI was averaged over three zones around the leases (0–1, 1–3 and 3–5 km). The series were compared with IMD gridded rainfall, ERA5 2 m air temperature, and the opencast, underground and total coal production reported in SCCL annual reports for 2004–05 to 2024–25.

All three zones greened, and the zone next to the mines stayed the least green. Rainfall and temperature showed no significant trend. Production correlated strongly with NDVI in raw form, but both production series are nearly linear in time, and once the linear trend was removed from both sides no production correlation exceeded |r| = 0.28. A regression on year alone explained 65% of NDVI variance, and adding rainfall and opencast output lowered adjusted R². The record shows regional greening that company-wide production totals cannot explain, and it shows why production-based correlations in mining studies need to be detrended before they are read as cause.

Project at a glance

Study area
Nine SCCL mining leases, Godavari valley coalfield, Peddapalli and Mancherial districts, Telangana. Zones out to 5 km cover about 838 km².
Period
NDVI 2005–2026 (22 years); matched analysis 2005–2025 (21 years)
Imagery
Landsat 5 TM, 7 ETM+, 8 OLI, 9 OLI-2, Collection 2 Level-2, path 143 row 47
Other data
IMD 0.25° daily rainfall, ERA5 2 m temperature, SCCL annual production, TGRAC lease boundaries
Methods
NDVI zonal means, Mann–Kendall and Sen's slope, anomalies, Pearson and Spearman correlation, linear detrending, first differences, OLS regression
Tools
QGIS, Python (pandas, NumPy, xarray, pyMannKendall, SciPy, statsmodels, Matplotlib)
