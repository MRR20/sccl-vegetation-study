Landsat scenes

All scenes come from WRS-2 path 143, row 47. One scene was taken per year from January or February, the middle of the dry season. Crops are harvested or mature then, deciduous trees still carry leaves, and cloud is rare. Using the same season every year means the series compares like with like. Scenes with more than 10% cloud were rejected, and every scene used had 2% or less.

Landsat 5 covers 2004–2011, its last year of regular imaging. Landsat 7 fills 2012 and 2013, before Landsat 8 dry-season data existed. Landsat 7 images after May 2003 have wedge-shaped gaps from the failed scan line corrector; those pixels are skipped when zone means are taken. The 2004 scene was processed but left out of the trend series so that every NDVI year from 2005 has its preceding monsoon in the rainfall record.

Rainfall

IMD daily gridded rainfall at 0.25° resolution was read from the yearly NetCDF files for 2004–2025. Grid cells between 79.223°E and 79.719°E and between 18.552°N and 19.137°N were averaged for each day, then summed over the calendar year for annual rainfall and over June to September for monsoon rainfall. Monsoon rainfall averaged 988.2 mm over 2005–2025, with a low of 455 mm in 2004 and a high of 1522 mm in 2022.

Temperature

ERA5 2 m air temperature for 2004–2025 was cropped to the same box, averaged over it, converted from kelvin to °C, and averaged by year and over January and February. The January–February mean matches the dates of the Landsat scenes.

Coal production

Opencast, underground and total production for each financial year were entered from the SCCL annual reports in lakh tonnes. These are company-wide figures, covering all SCCL areas and not only the nine leases.

Correction applied

The 2004–05 total had been entered as 361.38 lakh tonnes, the same figure as 2005–06. Its opencast (223.29) and underground (129.74) values add to 353.03, which is the value used throughout this site.
