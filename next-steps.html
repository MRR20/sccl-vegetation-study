Chapter 7

Limitations and next steps

What the current analysis cannot answer, and the work planned to answer it.

Limitations

  • One scene per year. A single January or February date captures the dry season but is sensitive to crop timing; scene dates ranged from 11 January to 27 February.
  • Mixed sensors. TM, ETM+ and OLI have different band widths and no cross-sensor adjustment was applied. Part of the step around 2012–2014 may come from the sensor change.
  • Landsat 7 gaps. The 2012 and 2013 scenes have scan-line gaps, so fewer pixels count in those years.
  • Company-wide production. SCCL totals cover all its areas, so they measure mining at the nine leases only loosely.
  • Short series. With 21 matched years, only correlations above about 0.43 reach significance, and many tests were run.
  • Coarse climate data. The IMD and ERA5 grids are about 0.25°, too coarse to tell the zones apart.
  • Zone means hide mixed land cover. Each zone mixes pits, dumps, reclaimed land, farmland, settlements, water and forest, which can change in opposite directions.
  • Lease interiors in the 0–1 km zone. The zone includes the mine floor, so its lower NDVI does not by itself show an effect outside the leases.

Planned work

  1. Pixel-level trend map

    Run Mann–Kendall and Sen's slope on every 30 m pixel to map where vegetation increased, decreased or stayed stable. Overlaying the map on imagery shows whether greening follows canals, dumps or forest. A ready script is in Downloads.

  2. Rings outside the leases

    Compute the 0–1 km statistics for land outside the lease boundaries only, to test whether mining suppresses vegetation beyond the boundary. The pixel script reports this split.

  3. Cross-sensor harmonisation

    Adjust Landsat 5 and 7 NDVI to match Landsat 8 using published coefficients, and use several scenes per season to build composites.

  4. Mine-level disturbance

    Map bare and pit area per lease per year from the imagery, for example with LandTrendr (Xiao et al., 2020), and use it in place of company production.

  5. Land cover separation

    Classify land cover for selected years, following Garai and Narayana (2018), and compute trends separately for farmland, forest, reclaimed land and active mine surfaces.

  6. Driver data and a combined model

    Collect irrigated area, plantation and reclamation records, groundwater or soil moisture, and fit one model with these drivers and year together.
